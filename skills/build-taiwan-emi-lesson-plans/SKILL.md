# Build Taiwan EMI Lesson Plans

## Workflow

1. Read the supplied lesson-plan and EMI checklist templates completely. Preserve their table structure, labels, and order unless the user asks for a redesign.
2. Identify subject content first. English is a learning medium and disciplinary tool, not a replacement for subject rigor.
3. Write measurable ILOs as action verb plus disciplinary knowledge/competence. Align teaching, student evidence, and assessment.
4. Design one coherent ODIR loop: orient to the outcome, diagnose/declare the task, interact with meaningful content, and review against evidence.
5. Add disciplinary English: a small keyword set, reusable sentence patterns, signposting, and teacher talk.
6. Reduce anxiety with multimodal input, chunking, wait time, rehearsal, pair work, sentence frames, and planned translanguaging. Do not require full-English output before students have conceptual and linguistic support.
7. Balance lower- and higher-order cognition. Include a public or authentic product when appropriate.
8. Self-check every plan with `references/emi-checklist.md`; fill the provided checklist and identify at least one next improvement.
9. Preserve the original file. Save the sample as a new DOCX and visually render it when a Word-compatible renderer is available.

## Cross-subject design

For English x Guidance x Chinese, prefer one shared product. A strong pattern is: Chinese text analysis provides the idea and evidence; Guidance provides self-knowledge and decision tools; English provides audience, structure, and public communication.

## Output requirements

- Unit, grade, subjects, issues, competencies, and ILOs
- Period-by-period ODIR procedures and timing
- Subject content and disciplinary English
- Three or more teaching supports
- Cognition levels, materials, formative evidence, rubric, differentiation
- Completed EMI self-check with concrete notes
